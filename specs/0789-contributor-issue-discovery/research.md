# Contributor discovery research, 30 October 2026

GitHub documents labels, topic pages and Explore as discovery routes. Its label guidance says
`good first issue` can increase the likelihood of surfacing approachable issues; it promises no
ranking or traffic. Its contributor guide recommends checking maintenance activity and describes
documentation and tests as contribution paths.

Primary sources:
- https://docs.github.com/en/communities/setting-up-your-project-for-healthy-contributions/encouraging-helpful-contributions-to-your-project-with-labels
- https://docs.github.com/en/get-started/exploring-projects-on-github/finding-ways-to-contribute-to-open-source-on-github
- https://docs.github.com/en/issues/tracking-your-work-with-issues/using-issues/filtering-and-searching-issues-and-pull-requests

Measured: Space Radar already has topics, onboarding guides and skill-filtered starter links.
A GitHub search for open, unassigned, closing-PR-unlinked starter issues returned 269 before
reservation exclusions. This is inventory, not traffic or a guarantee of availability.
GitHub documents linked:pr as a closing reference; it cannot detect every plain PR mention.

Inference: improving issue suitability and avoiding duplicate work is a better-supported small
intervention than activity bumps. Contributor growth remains unmeasured.

## Measurement after merge

Compare 14 days after merge with the preceding 14 days: distinct outside authors opening PRs
referencing starter issues, and the share receiving a maintainer response within 48 hours.
Exclude bots and maintainers using existing contributor-generation rules. Manually check unlinked
references. Record reports of tasks already taken or blocked. Search impressions are unmeasured.

Concurrent releases, promotion and inventory changes confound attribution; report descriptive
results, not causal claims. No scheduled job or analytics service is added.
